# Classic BrickBattle tools and demo hazards

The selected launcher is Roblox's public Classic Rocket Launcher model **47637** (model SHA-256 `4761c56eb065dce6793b6b5709de7d55227462e6506ed14414bde2b604925f55`). It contains the gray launcher, Tool Grip, mesh and texture references, cursors, client script, projectile script, and sounds. Public mesh `12221651` SHA-256 is `6f99cf96f5e15ac04ed8d1e20ec341acabf724111e8916d2b8730af239f789d6`; texture `12224070` is `998d9f47f41612f674f3d269143bc727281483cf0e5b5dff65ba37dc42c2fc38`, icon `90021376` is `14afa71c8a7d280c7358b0e4a06524b29ac09bb7e5a406be0297ae7de7b57491`. Public whoosh `12222095` and collide `12221984` are embedded as OGG audio. The launcher mesh was converted from version-1 geometry and quantized for WebGL; maximum absolute conversion error is under 0.00005.

The public script specifies projectile speed **60**, reload **3 seconds**, lifetime **30 seconds**, blast radius **8**, damage **60**, and a mass-scaled force value **1000**. Its current model uses a different missile mesh: the blue 1×1×3 studded brick here is a deliberate user-requested older-style projectile, not a claim that this exact model script used that brick. Its swept collision, blast impulses and sounds are browser adaptations, not recovered Roblox `Touched`, Explosion or solver code. First-contact checks include the demo dummy, world and wall bricks; loose walls are not simply deleted. Damage does not reproduce all Roblox team, ownership or force-field semantics.

The public Classic Trowel Tool **47582** uses mesh `12221793`, texture `12229499` and icon `12224320`. Its source calls for a 12×4 wall, 0.04 seconds between placements, five seconds after building before reactivation and 24-second brick cleanup. Its original script places at a mouse target and snaps the orientation. Here, per the user's correction, a twelve-brick wall starts directly in front of the player and faces their heading. Bricks receive independent browser-simulated velocities and spin when hit by a rocket or sword; their collision and physical response remain approximations.

The explosion renderer uses decoded PNG versions of owner-hash-matched 1145 textures `explosion01_core_main.dds` (`afc096fca6e0f50c151135b6c4da186cb8098fb2e99c3f61596028fea980d982`), `explosion01_smoke_main.dds` (`53606e2ab42ab66efb9934bda37c00af322850ff322358f7db50c2884ddd1e30`) and `explosion01_shockwave_main.dds` (`6b186021c6d3ae42aa1f69cc3c80dbbe761f677124a7783866c1a551e272a48f`). Billboard motion, blending, size and lifetime are authored approximations, not reconstructed particle emitter behavior.

The orange damage and red kill bricks, environment, targeting adapter and player controller are authored demo elements. Original place contents, Roblox's collision solver and active multiplayer services have not been recovered. Previously included rocket `32356064` is retained only as an unused historical file; it is no longer loaded or displayed.

At the user’s request, trowel bricks are now independent, unanchored simulated bodies from creation, fall against the stairs, and react to player movement. The user-requested launcher change inflicts lethal demo damage and a strong outward impulse; the public script’s historical damage is not represented as this new value. The shared rotating-box contacts, friction, settling, collisions and impulses remain authored browser approximations, not the Roblox solver.

The 47637 public Rocket.lua sets BlastPressure to zero and applies BodyForce = direction × 1000 × part mass for 0.1 seconds to loose/dead parts; living Humanoids are damaged separately. This adapter now applies equivalent constant 1000-stud/s² acceleration to independently moving bricks for 0.1 seconds, using rotated-box radius contacts. Per user request, lethal damage and modest 18-stud/s character knockback are separate overrides, not claims from that script. Browser contacts/settling and network synchronization remain approximate.

At user request, remote character boxes are included in swept rocket contacts. The browser-only lethal override remains 1000 damage; the visible character impulse is tuned to 50 studs/s (up from 35) and the blast radius is 14 studs for the larger rocket explosion. Loose bricks retain the public script’s 0.1-second, mass-scaled force behavior. This is not a recovered Roblox solver.
The public script spawns at the handle's local `(5,0,0)`; because the recreated arm/tool weld can aim that point below the ground, the browser falls back to an above-ground, forward-aimed muzzle point when needed. The original live weld has not been recovered.

For user-requested lethal hits, each newly detached body part now receives an outward force over 0.1 seconds based on its own position, following the public script's per-part direction/timing. Its 350-stud/s² browser acceleration is a reduced 35% tuning of that script's 1000 to respect the earlier request for less force on humans; the native solver has not been recovered.
The player can stand on each loose brick using a vertical query against its rotated box; this is an authored contact adapter, not native Roblox physics. Sleeping bricks still wake under pushes and blasts.

The General's .45 is public Roblox tool 97885508. The actual tool XML, source scripts, mesh 97886770, texture 97888197, icon 97888232 and audio 97848313/97848255 were downloaded. Its source sets 40 damage, 0.5 s fire interval, 6 rounds, 3.7 s reload and 250 stud range. The source sets 40 damage once per bullet; it destroys the Left Hip, Right Hip or Left Shoulder when that leg or arm is hit and removes the detached piece after ten seconds. A Right Arm hit moves equipped Tools to Workspace, and a Head hit knocks off Hats. The browser approximation models the three detachable R6 limbs, ten-second cleanup and a dropped equipped tool. It has no rendered Hats/accessories to knock off, and missing legs do not reproduce Roblox humanoid movement. Multiplayer hit/loot validation and visual trajectories are approximate; they are not the original Roblox engine. The script's apparently unscoped `hit` is set by its `CastRay` function.

User-directed loose-body tuning uses density multiplier 4, nearly inelastic floor/box contacts, stronger angular damping and mass-sensitive walking pushes to reduce bouncing and perpetual spin. These are browser feel settings, not recovered Roblox values. Historical Roblox guidance described legacy density 1, friction 0.3 and elasticity 0.5, with later plastic density around 0.7: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/live-upcoming-physical-properties-and-partmaterial-changes/19608
